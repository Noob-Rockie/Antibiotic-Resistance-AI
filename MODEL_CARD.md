## Intended use

A research and educational **clinical decision-support prototype**. It estimates the probability that a laboratory would report an identified isolate as non-susceptible to a given antibiotic, and presents that estimate with its explanation, its evidence base and its guardrails to a qualified clinician during the window between organism identification and the AST result.

The intended user is a clinician or antimicrobial-stewardship pharmacist who retains full decision authority.

## Out-of-scope use

- **Autonomous prescribing of any kind.** The system has no mechanism to order a drug and must never be given one.
- Replacing antimicrobial susceptibility testing.
- Specimen types other than urine, blood and respiratory.
- Paediatric or neonatal patients.
- Any health system other than the one the data came from, without recalibration and local validation.
- Predicting clinical outcome, cure or mortality.
- Dose selection. Dosage text is relayed verbatim from the FDA label and is never computed.
- Cost-driven agent selection. NADAC prices are US pharmacy acquisition costs per unit, shown for reference only.

## Limitations

1. **Single centre, single country.** Resistance epidemiology is intensely local.
2. **Panel-selection bias.** An isolate is only labelled for the agents the laboratory chose to test, and that choice depends on the organism and specimen. The model learns the pattern *conditional on being tested*.
3. **Labels are laboratory categories, not clinical outcomes.** Breakpoint definitions also changed during the study period.
4. **Partial history coverage.** Prior resistance exists for 37.8% of orders and prior medications for 69.7%. Care delivered outside this health system is invisible, so absence of a record is not absence of exposure.
5. **No genomic data.** The source tables contain no gene, mutation or AMR marker. The genomic module is implemented but inactive and the model makes no genotypic claims.
6. **De-identified sex.** Coded 0/1 with no legend, so no sex-specific claim can be made or audited.
7. **Temporal drift.** Temporal-holdout performance is below the random-patient figure; a deployed model would need monitoring and periodic refitting.
8. **No comorbidity, weight, severity score or source-control information** exists in the data, so the model cannot account for any of it.

Sources of bias that cannot be removed by modelling:

- **The training labels encode historical practice.** Which agents were tested, and on whom, reflects past clinical decisions. A model fitted to that can perpetuate whatever inequity it contained.
- **Patients with more prior contact have richer histories**, so the model has more to work with for them. Patients seen elsewhere, or seen for the first time, get a prediction driven almost entirely by organism and specimen.
- **Cohort composition skews urinary** (urine is the largest specimen type by a wide margin), which shapes which organisms and agents the model has seen most.

## Clinical safety limitations

- The output is a probability that a *laboratory* would call an isolate non-susceptible. It is not a prediction of whether a patient will get better.
- **Under-prediction of resistance leads to an ineffective antibiotic.** At the deployed threshold the model misses 45.46% of non-susceptible results. Operating points for asymmetric costs are published in `docs/MODEL_EVALUATION.md` §6.
- **Over-prediction of resistance drives escalation** to broader agents, which accelerates the resistance the tool exists to help with. Both error directions are reported; neither is optimised away silently.
- **Automation bias is the principal human-factors risk.** The interface never shows a single answer, always shows the evidence base and confidence band, ranks flagged options visibly rather than hiding them, and requires an explicit recorded clinician decision.
- The model has no knowledge of allergy, pregnancy, renal or hepatic function, drug interactions, source control, or local formulary. Allergy is handled only as a clinician-entered flag.

## Maintenance

- Retrain and recalibrate when local resistance prevalence shifts, when breakpoints are revised, or at least annually.
- Re-run `python scripts/00_verify_raw_integrity.py` before any retraining to confirm the source data has not silently changed.
- Monitor the temporal-holdout metric, not the random-split one; it is the one that degrades first under drift.
